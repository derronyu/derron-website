# Derron Yu Brand Guidelines

Version 1.0 — August 2026

## Brand Idea

### Analytical Optimism

Derron Yu's identity should make rigorous thinking feel energetic, accessible, and useful. The brand sits at the intersection of finance, research, sustainability, and ambitious problem-solving. It should look credible in a professional setting without becoming another conservative finance portfolio.

The central visual tension is **editorial authority + optimistic experimentation**:

- Editorial scale and portraiture make Derron the clear protagonist.
- A visible grid communicates structure, evidence, and analytical discipline.
- Selective color introduces curiosity, momentum, and range.
- Warm, tactile details keep the identity personal rather than institutional.

**Positioning statement:** Derron Yu is a multidisciplinary finance professional who turns market, policy, and sustainability questions into clear, evidence-led decisions.

**Short brand line:** Markets, ideas, and impact—made legible.

## Brand Principles

### 1. Evidence has personality

Show the work: outcomes, constraints, methods, and lessons. Data should feel designed and understandable, not dropped into a generic dashboard.

### 2. Serious, never stiff

The identity should signal professional judgment without relying on navy blue, stock-market imagery, or corporate clichés.

### 3. Breadth with a point of view

Finance, policy, sustainability, sports business, and research belong together when organized around Derron's ability to find patterns and make decisions.

### 4. Human at every scale

Use direct language, candid photography, and moments of warmth. The site should feel like meeting a thoughtful person, not reading a résumé database.

### 5. Optimism is a discipline

Bright accents represent possibilities, but layouts remain ordered. Color should clarify hierarchy and categories rather than decorate at random.

## Audience

The primary audience is hiring managers, finance and consulting professionals, collaborators, and academic peers. They should understand three things within the first minute:

1. Derron's professional focus and current direction.
2. The quality and range of his thinking.
3. Where to find evidence through selected work, research, and outcomes.

## Visual Direction

The design language is an **editorial field notebook for modern finance**. It uses a large personal introduction, modular evidence cards, fine rules, restrained typography, and a warm paper-like canvas.

### Design expression

- Use visible grid logic, confident color blocking, compact project cards, and crisp outlines to communicate structure and momentum.
- Combine portrait-led storytelling with oversized editorial type, elegant serif contrast, and generous scale to make the experience feel personal.
- Keep the palette controlled, prioritize quantified outcomes, and organize work around finance, research, and impact.

## Logo and Wordmark

Use a typographic wordmark as the primary identity:

**DERRON YU**

- Set in the primary sans serif, uppercase, medium weight.
- Use tight tracking (`-0.04em`) and no decorative effects.
- Pair it with the custom **DY** monogram when space permits. The mark combines a square-height geometric `D` with a broad semicircular right side and a separate inverted triangle in the upper-right.
- The compact **DY** monogram may be used for the favicon and social avatar.
- On light backgrounds, use an Ink `D` with a Signal Coral triangle. On Ink backgrounds, switch the `D` to Soft Paper and retain the Signal Coral triangle.

Do not create a finance-themed symbol, candlestick chart, leaf-and-dollar icon, or elaborate signature mark. The work and the typography should carry the identity.

### Clear space

Keep clear space equal to the cap height of the `D` around every side of the wordmark. Never place the mark directly over a visually busy image.

## Color System

The palette is warm, high-contrast, and grounded. **Paper** and **Ink** should account for roughly 80% of every page. Accent colors are signals, not backgrounds for every section.

| Token | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#F2EAD3` | Primary canvas; warm and human |
| Soft Paper | `#FBF7EC` | Reading surfaces and light cards |
| Ink | `#17231C` | Primary text, borders, dark sections |
| Moss | `#315843` | Finance, sustainability, and grounded emphasis |
| Signal Coral | `#F06F4F` | Calls to action, key phrases, active states |
| Citron | `#DDEB72` | Data highlights, labels, optimistic moments |
| Aubergine | `#75485D` | Research and long-form editorial work |
| Lake | `#5573A9` | Strategy, technology, and secondary charts |

### Usage rules

- Default text is Ink on Paper or Soft Paper.
- Large dark sections use Paper on Ink.
- Signal Coral is the primary action color and should appear once or twice per viewport.
- Citron works best for small labels, selected states, and data points; avoid long text on it.
- Moss, Aubergine, and Lake can categorize projects, but category meaning must stay consistent.
- All text and interactive color pairings must meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.

### Avoid

- Pure white and pure black as the dominant pair.
- Generic financial navy with bright cyan.
- Gradients used as filler.
- Giving every card a different color without semantic meaning.

## Typography

### Primary pairing

- **Newsreader** — display headlines, pull quotes, and editorial moments.
- **Manrope** — body copy, navigation, labels, buttons, numbers, and data.

Both families are open source and suitable for web use. Newsreader provides considered editorial character; Manrope supplies clean geometry and strong numerals.

### Type scale

| Style | Desktop | Mobile | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | `clamp(4.5rem, 10vw, 9rem)` | `3.25–4.5rem` | Newsreader, 0.88–0.95 line height |
| H1 | `clamp(3.5rem, 7vw, 6.5rem)` | `2.75–3.75rem` | Newsreader |
| H2 | `clamp(2.25rem, 4vw, 4rem)` | `2–2.75rem` | Newsreader |
| H3 | `1.5–2rem` | `1.35–1.65rem` | Manrope semibold |
| Body large | `1.25rem` | `1.125rem` | 1.45 line height |
| Body | `1rem` | `1rem` | 1.6 line height |
| Label | `0.72–0.8rem` | same | Uppercase, `0.08em` tracking |

### Typographic behavior

- Use the serif for ideas and the sans serif for facts.
- Keep paragraphs to approximately 55–70 characters per line.
- Prefer sentence case. Reserve all caps for short labels and the wordmark.
- Use tabular numerals for dates, percentages, and results.
- Use italic serif sparingly to add human emphasis inside large headlines.

## Grid, Spacing, and Shape

### Grid

- Base layout: 12 columns on desktop, 6 on tablet, 4 on mobile.
- Maximum content width: `1440px`; standard page gutters: `32–48px` desktop and `20px` mobile.
- Let hero typography and selected media break the grid; keep body content aligned to it.
- A subtle background grid may appear in heroes and project indexes at 4–7% opacity.

### Spacing

Use an 8px base system with preferred steps of `8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 144`.

- Section spacing: `96–144px` desktop, `64–96px` mobile.
- Card padding: `24–32px`.
- Keep dense data inside cards and generous breathing room between narrative sections.

### Shape language

- Borders: 1–2px Ink, used deliberately to make the system feel precise.
- Corners: mostly square or subtly rounded (`0–8px`).
- One signature shape is allowed: an exaggerated lower-right radius on featured cards, echoing a turning page or expanding possibility.
- Avoid pill-shaped containers except for small status tags.

## Photography and Imagery

Photography should feel observant and real rather than aspirational-corporate.

### Portrait direction

- Use a full- or three-quarter-body environmental portrait with direct eye contact.
- Prefer natural window light and warm neutral surroundings.
- Wardrobe should be textured and contemporary—olive, charcoal, cream, rust—not a generic suit against a studio backdrop.
- Leave negative space for oversized typography to overlap the image edge.

### Project imagery

- Lead with artifacts: annotated models, presentation details, paper excerpts, charts, and team moments.
- Crop decisively; do not display full slide screenshots when a meaningful detail tells the story better.
- Pair every project with a concrete caption explaining what the viewer is seeing and why it matters.
- Use a light, consistent grain treatment across photography if needed; never apply heavy color filters.

### Data graphics

- Use Ink as the baseline and one semantic accent per chart.
- Prefer direct labels over detached legends.
- Remove ornamental gridlines; retain only the structure needed to read the finding.
- Every chart needs a one-sentence takeaway, source, and accessible text alternative.

## Iconography

Use simple, custom line icons with a 1.5–2px stroke and squared or gently rounded terminals. Arrows should feel directional and useful, not decorative.

- Use northeast arrows for external links and project entry points.
- Use the DY monogram only for brand identification, not as a decorative bullet or repeated page motif.
- Avoid mixed icon packs, filled social icons, and emoji as permanent interface elements.

## Voice and Writing

### Voice attributes

- **Clear:** state the conclusion early.
- **Curious:** show the question behind the work.
- **Grounded:** support claims with evidence and context.
- **Warm:** write in first person and sound like a real conversation.
- **Forward-looking:** connect past work to the problems Derron wants to solve next.

### Writing pattern for projects

Use this sequence:

1. **Question** — What problem or decision was at stake?
2. **Approach** — What did Derron analyze, build, or lead?
3. **Evidence** — What methods, constraints, or inputs mattered?
4. **Outcome** — What changed, was learned, or was achieved?
5. **Reflection** — What would Derron carry into the next challenge?

### Example hero copy

> I study markets, policy, and sustainability to make complex decisions clearer.

Supporting copy:

> I'm Derron Yu, a finance professional and researcher working across investment strategy, sustainable growth, and data-led problem solving.

### Avoid

- “Welcome to my website.”
- Inflated claims such as “visionary,” “world-class,” or “results-driven.”
- Long autobiographical paragraphs before showing any work.
- Skill percentages without an objective basis.
- Repeating résumé bullets without adding context or reflection.

## Site Experience

### Recommended information architecture

1. **Home** — positioning, portrait, selected work, proof points, short biography, contact.
2. **Work** — case competitions and professional projects as filterable case studies.
3. **Research** — papers with abstracts, themes, and readable previews.
4. **About** — career timeline, education, interests, and values.
5. **Contact** — direct invitation, email, and LinkedIn.

“Skills” should become supporting evidence inside projects and the About page rather than remain a standalone list.

### Homepage sequence

1. Portrait-led hero with a precise one-sentence proposition.
2. Three proof points: current focus, selected result, and multidisciplinary range.
3. Modular selected-work grid with differentiated card sizes.
4. Short “how I think” section connecting finance, policy, and sustainability.
5. Career snapshot or timeline.
6. Direct contact invitation on an Ink background.

### Project cards

Each card should include:

- Category and year.
- Project title.
- One-sentence problem or outcome.
- One relevant artifact or data detail.
- Clear entry arrow and a descriptive link label.

Card size should reflect importance, not chronology. Use at most three accent colors within one viewport.

## Motion

Motion should reveal structure and direction.

- On load, reveal the hero in a short sequence: label, headline, portrait, then supporting proof points.
- Use 400–700ms transitions with confident easing; avoid springy or playful physics.
- Project cards may shift 4–8px and reveal a directional arrow on hover.
- A very slow background-grid drift or chart-line draw may be used once as a signature moment.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and preserve all meaning without animation.
- Avoid scroll hijacking, custom cursors, parallax on body copy, and constant looping effects.

## Accessibility and Inclusive Design

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard requirements.
- Keep focus states visible with a 2px Signal Coral outline and offset.
- Never convey project category or chart meaning through color alone.
- Write useful alt text for project artifacts and leave decorative images with empty alt text.
- Maintain a minimum 44×44px interactive target.
- Keep body text at 16px or larger and avoid justified text.
- Provide descriptive PDF titles, file sizes, and HTML summaries before downloads.

## Quality Bar

Before a page is considered on-brand, confirm that:

- Its main idea is clear without scrolling.
- At least one artifact or outcome proves the claims being made.
- Paper and Ink remain dominant, with accents used semantically.
- Serif and sans typography have distinct jobs.
- The layout has one intentional break from the grid, not many accidental ones.
- Photography feels personal and specific.
- Motion clarifies hierarchy and fully degrades for reduced-motion users.
- The page is equally understandable with images, color, and animation removed.
